Real-World Offensive Attack Vectors

1. Dictionary Attack

How Attackers Use It: Attackers test wordlists containing millions of leaked breach passwords against authentication targets.

Why It Is Dangerous: Completely bypasses mathematical entropy calculations; even long passwords are cracked instantly if listed in breach corpus databases.

2. Brute Force Attack

How Attackers Use It: Systematically testing all character combinations (a-z, A-Z, 0-9, symbols) sequentially until matching the password hash.

Why It Is Dangerous: High-end GPU rigs can test hundreds of billions of combinations per second. Short passwords (<10 chars) collapse rapidly.

3. Hybrid Attack

How Attackers Use It: Combines dictionary words with automated rule modifications—prepending numbers, appending calendar years, and leetspeak symbols.

Why It Is Dangerous: Cracks passwords like Summer2024! or P@ssword123 instantly by mutating base dictionary words.

4. Credential Stuffing

How Attackers Use It: Automated bots attempt email/password pairs stolen from prior data breaches across unrelated enterprise portals.

Why It Is Dangerous: Reusing passwords makes multiple personal accounts vulnerable when a single service suffers a breach.

5. Mask Attack

How Attackers Use It: Targets human behavioral patterns using structural masks, such as ?u?l?l?l?l?d?d?d?d?s (Uppercase + 4 Lowercase + 4 Digits + Symbol).

Why It Is Dangerous: Reduces brute force search spaces by orders of magnitude by testing predictable character structures.
